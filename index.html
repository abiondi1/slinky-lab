Slinky harmonics lab

One end of a stretched slinky is shaken side to side while the other end is held still. At certain frequencies the slinky settles into a standing wave with 1, 2, 3 or more loops. Use the timer and cycle counter to find the frequency, the orange arrows to measure wavelength, and the graph to compare wave shapes.

The slinky

Presets:

Changing the slinky resets it to still. Preset values are measured slinkies: classic metal 215.5 g, k ≈ 0.69 N/m; plastic 48.7 g, k ≈ 0.22 N/m (Cross and Wheatland, American Journal of Physics, 2012).

Answer key

# of loopsCycles in 10 sLength of one loop (m)Frequency (Hz)Wavelength (m)Frequency × wavelength (m/s)

Pattern: frequency goes up by the same step each time (1×, 2×, 3×, 4× the first), wavelength shrinks (2L, L, 2L/3, L/2), and their product stays the same. That constant is the wave speed in this slinky. Student counts within about ±1 cycle are normal, since they count whole cycles. The slinky is modelled as a zero-length spring (tension proportional to stretched length), which is close to real slinky behaviour; that is why the frequencies don't change with length, only the wavelengths do.
